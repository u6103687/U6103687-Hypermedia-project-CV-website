**CV website - Marta Serra**

Hypermedia project, part 1: a single-page CV and portfolio website.

**Project analysis**

**User profile**

The owner of the CV is Marta Serra, the same persona I used as the target audience in the product card exercise: a 24-year-old designer from Barcelona who loves sneaker and streetwear culture. She has now finished her degree and is looking for her first junior job, so her product card has become the first project of her portfolio.

The final user of the website is the person who visits it to decide whether to hire her:

Laia Ferrer, 35, creative director at a small branding studio in Barcelona

- Work: her studio works for sneaker shops, streetwear and vintage brands, and she is hiring a junior visual designer.

- Device: she reviews applications on her MacBook at the studio, so the page is designed for desktop screens.

- Context: she opens Marta's link from an e-mail or a LinkedIn application, together with about thirty other candidates. She spends one or two minutes on each one before deciding who to call.

- What she expects to find:
  - Who the candidate is and whether she is available: presentation card with name, role and the "Open to junior roles" badge.
  - Real work, seen at a glance: Selected work, with a link to the prototype.
  - Tools she can use from day one (Figma, Illustrator, Photoshop, After Effects) and languages for international clients: About and skills.
  - Previous experience, even if it is small: Experience and education.
  - A fast way to get in touch or keep the CV: e-mail, LinkedIn, InfoJobs and the Download CV button.

**Information architecture**

The website is a single page whose order follows the hierarchy of frames in the Figma file: Menú, Presentació, Projectes, About, Exp CV and Contact + final. Each frame became a section, and the sections are numbered from 02 to 05 so the visitor always knows where she is. The presentation works as an unnumbered cover (the "01").

The menu comes first, with Marta's initials on the left and links to the three main sections on the right. The Contact link is a black button because getting in touch is the final goal of the page.

The presentation answers the first question, "who is she?", in a few seconds. It is designed as a product label (Model, Style, Edition) with her name, role, city and availability, plus two actions: see her work or download the CV.

Selected work goes straight after because, for a designer, the work is the strongest proof of what she can do; it is where a recruiter decides. The three cards share the same structure: image, title, year and tool, tag and link to the prototype.

Once the visitor is interested, About and skills gives more detail: a short bio on the left and the skills on the right, grouped by category as tags that can be scanned quickly.

Experience and education shows her path as a timeline in reverse chronological order, with the current job highlighted in blue.

Finally, the contact block closes the page on a dark background as the place to act: e-mail, LinkedIn, InfoJobs and the CV button again. The order goes from the most decisive information to the least.

**Visual design**

The concept is to present the CV as a product sheet, continuing the sneaker product card from the previous exercise. Marta appears as a "model" with a style and an edition, like a shoe in a shop.

Colour: I kept the approach from the first exercise: a light grey background so the content stands out, and near-black for text, borders and the main button. There is only one accent colour, a strong blue, used for section numbers, links, the main buttons and the current job. Green appears only in the availability badge, in the same way that red was used only for the discount in the product card. The contact block is dark to mark the end of the page.

Typography: Libre Caslon Display is used for all the text. It is an elegant editorial serif that relates to fashion magazines and keeps the retro feeling that Limelight gave to the product card, while being easier to read in longer texts. IBM Plex Serif is reused from the first exercise for the numbers, so the section numbers stand out from the rest of the text.

Layout: the page is designed for desktop, with wide side margins, three project cards in a row and the same few spacing values repeated everywhere. The cards and buttons have thick black borders, heavier on the right and bottom so they work as a hard shadow, plus slightly rounded corners and pill-shaped tags. This gives the page a tactile, printed-label look that fits a young designer.

Reused from the first exercise: the grey background, the black call-to-action button, IBM Plex Serif for numbers, the single accent colour and the product card itself, which is the first project.
